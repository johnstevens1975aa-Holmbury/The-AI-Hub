Cause, Effect and Simple Sensors

How intelligent systems learn, operate and produce outputs

📅 Teaching sequence⭐ HPL: analysing · linking · meta-thinking🌍 SDG: innovation · equality · peace🤖 AI Literacy

Learning intent

  • I can explain the key AI idea in this unit.
  • I can apply it to a classroom or real-world scenario.
  • I can use evidence to judge whether the output, design or decision is reliable, fair and safe.

Concrete example

Learners examine a simple example set, predict what the model will do with a tricky new example, then explain which feature or pattern influenced the output in Cause, Effect and Simple Sensors.

Teacher model

Think aloud: define the problem, identify the evidence or data, test the response, name one risk, then improve the prompt, design, rule or safeguard.

Student task

Complete a claim → evidence → improvement response. Then add one RAG judgement and one next-step target.

Common misconception

AI understands like a person.

Correction: AI systems produce outputs from data, rules, training and probability. Pupils should name the data, objective, model or rule behind the output.

Quick check

  1. What made the AI system produce that output?
  2. What evidence would make you trust or reject it?
  3. What safeguard or improvement would make this better?

Assessment rubric

Emerging

Names the AI idea with prompts and uses one keyword accurately.

Proficient

Explains the idea independently, applies it to the task and justifies a decision with evidence.

Advanced

Evaluates trade-offs, improves the solution or judgement, and transfers the idea to a new context.

Support

Use vocabulary cards, sentence stems, partially completed examples, paired talk and visual prompts.

Stretch

Ask pupils to compare two designs or judgements, identify trade-offs and defend the stronger choice.

Evidence to collect

Annotated task, prompt iteration, design sketch, audit note, RAG response, short written judgement or portfolio extract.

AI understands like a person.
